## The Flashcard Problem

Flashcards are the most popular vocabulary learning tool in existence. They are also one of the least efficient—in their basic form.

A word on a card, translated into your language, reviewed until you can recall it: this teaches recognition. It does not teach you to *use* the word naturally in speech or writing.

You know what this looks like in practice: you have studied "eloquent" twelve times on a flashcard. You could pick it out on a multiple-choice test. But you would never produce it in conversation.

## What Actually Builds Productive Vocabulary

**Contextual learning**. Meet a word in a sentence, not in isolation. Better: meet it in five different sentences across a week. Each encounter adds another dimension to the word's meaning.

**Active use within 24 hours**. When you learn a new word, use it—in a sentence, in a text message, in your journal—within a day. This dramatically increases retention.

**Spaced repetition**. Review a word after one day, then three days, then a week, then a month. Most flashcard apps can do this for you if set correctly. What matters is the *spacing*, not the *repetition*.

**Collocation awareness**. Learn "make a decision", not just "decision". Learn "heavy rain", not just "heavy". Words live in predictable company. Learning those partners doubles what you can do with the word.

## A Practical System

1. Encounter a new word in something you read or hear
2. Write one sentence using it from memory
3. Note one word it commonly appears with
4. Add to a spaced repetition system (Anki is free)
5. Review at the intervals the system suggests

This takes about three minutes per word and produces results that flashcard drilling doesn't.

## How Many Words Do You Need?

For basic conversation: 2,000 word families.
For reading most texts comfortably: 5,000 word families.
For IELTS Band 7: approximately 4,000-5,000.

Most intermediate learners have passive recognition of far more than this—and active control of far fewer. The gap between knowing a word and using it is where most study time should go.
