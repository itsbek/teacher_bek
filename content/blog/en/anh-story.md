## The Starting Point

Anh was not a weak English speaker. She had years of English at school, a degree she'd partly completed in English-medium instruction, and a very clear idea of what she needed: IELTS 7.0 overall, with 7.0 in Speaking.

Her first two attempts had given her 6.0 and 6.5. Close, but not close enough for the nursing college she'd applied to.

When she arrived, the first thing I did was listen to her speak for about ten minutes—no test conditions, just conversation. Her grammar was solid. Her vocabulary was decent. Her clarity was the issue.

## What We Worked On

The core problem was something I see often: Anh spoke *at* you rather than *with* you. She had memorised so many IELTS Part 2 structures that her speaking sounded scripted even when she wasn't reading from anything.

Examiners score coherence, fluency, and pronunciation. Scripted answers can score well on coherence but lose marks on fluency and authenticity.

We spent the first month doing something she found frustrating: talking without preparation. No topic cards, no timer, no structure. Just conversation. Topics she cared about. Her work, her family, things that annoyed her.

The goal was to reconnect speaking with thinking—to make it feel like *her* voice again, not an exam template.

## The Second Month

Once she had loosened up, we introduced structure back in—but this time built around her natural speaking patterns rather than imposed on them.

We also worked specifically on her final consonants (a common pattern for Vietnamese speakers) and on varying her intonation. Flat delivery costs marks on pronunciation, even when individual sounds are correct.

## The Result

Third attempt: 7.5 Speaking, 7.0 overall.

She messaged me the morning the results came out. I still have the screenshot.

## The Lesson

IELTS Speaking is partly an exam skill and partly a real communication skill. The students who do best treat it as the latter. The exam is designed to measure authentic communication—you can't fully fake your way to a 7 if your underlying communication isn't there.

But the underlying communication doesn't have to be fluent before you start. That's what the preparation is for.
