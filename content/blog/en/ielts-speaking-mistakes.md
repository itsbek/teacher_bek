## Band 6 Is a Trap

Most IELTS students I see aren't struggling because their English is poor. They're struggling because they've built habits that cap them at Band 6—and they don't know which habits.

Here are the five most common ones.

## 1. Using the Same Safe Vocabulary

Band 6 answers are accurate. Band 7+ answers are *precise*.

"I think it is very important" → "It's arguably the most critical factor"

The vocabulary shift doesn't require showing off. It requires expanding your default phrases beyond the safe ones you've used since secondary school.

**Fix**: Learn 3-5 higher-level synonyms for your most-used words. Use them in every practice session until they feel natural.

## 2. Answering the Question, Not Extending It

Examiners score *development*. A short accurate answer is worth less than a longer, more reasoned one.

Compare:
- "I prefer coffee because I like the taste." (Band 6)
- "I tend to prefer coffee—partly habit, partly the ritual of it. Tea feels more passive somehow." (Band 7)

**Fix**: After every answer, ask yourself: *did I explain why, or give an example?* If not, add one sentence.

## 3. Pronunciation that Drops at the End of Sentences

Vietnamese and Chinese speakers often drop volume and clarity on the final syllable of a sentence. Examiners catch this as mumbling, not accent.

**Fix**: Consciously stress the *last content word* of each sentence. It feels unnatural at first. That's fine.

## 4. Memorised Answers

Examiners hear thousands of Part 2 monologues about "a person who influenced me" or "a memorable journey." They recognise scripted answers.

**Fix**: Practise being flexible. Know your topics, not your scripts. The answer should sound like you're thinking—because you are.

## 5. Not Listening to Themselves

Most students have never heard themselves speak English. They're surprised by their own accent, pace, or filler words.

**Fix**: Record your Part 2 and Part 3 answers. Listen back once. Pick one thing to change next time.

---

Band 7 is achievable for most people sitting at Band 6. The ceiling is usually technique, not ability.
