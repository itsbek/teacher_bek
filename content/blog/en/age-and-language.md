## The Myth Worth Clearing Up

"Children learn languages better than adults."

This is partly true and mostly misleading. Children acquire *accent* more naturally—the phonological system is more plastic in early childhood. On almost every other measure—vocabulary acquisition rate, grammar comprehension, reading—adults learn faster.

What children have is time and immersion. A child in an English-speaking environment absorbs thousands of hours of input over years. Adults comparing themselves to that process are making an unfair comparison.

## What's Genuinely Harder After 30

**Accent**. If you've never spoken English before your twenties, reaching a native-like accent requires significant focused work. It's achievable, but it won't happen accidentally.

**Tolerance for ambiguity**. Children are comfortable not understanding everything. Adults find partial comprehension frustrating. That frustration can cause learners to withdraw from difficult input rather than push through it.

**Embarrassment in production**. Children say wrong things freely and move on. Adults worry about looking foolish. This slows speaking progress significantly.

## What's Easier

**Vocabulary**. Adults have richer conceptual knowledge. When you learn a new English word, you're often just attaching a label to a concept you already have. Children are building concepts and words simultaneously.

**Grammar logic**. Formal grammar instruction works better with adults. You can explain *why* something works. Children learn patterns without explanations.

**Metacognitive learning**. Adults know how they learn. They can choose methods, set schedules, notice their own errors, and self-correct in ways children can't.

**Motivation clarity**. Adult learners usually know exactly why they're studying. That specificity helps direct effort productively.

## What This Means Practically

If you're over 30 and starting English or improving it:

1. Don't aim for a flawless accent—aim for clarity
2. Spend more time on listening than most programmes suggest
3. Speak before you feel ready; imperfect production is still production
4. Use your knowledge advantage: read widely, connect new vocabulary to existing frameworks

Age changes how you learn, not whether you can.
