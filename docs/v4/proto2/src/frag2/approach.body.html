Approach

Why-first teaching

I explain what a piece of language is for before asking anyone to remember it. Here are five reasons, each with the research behind it.

scroll through the five →

reason one

Why explain the reason before the rule?

Because meaning is what memory holds on to. When a child knows what “the” is for, the rule stops being a sentence to memorise and becomes something they can work out again on their own.

Craik & Tulving, 1975 · Depth of processing

reason two

Why keep the load light?

Because working memory is small. A wall of tables fills it before anything can be learned. Fewer items, each with a reason, leave room to think.

Sweller, 1988 · Cognitive load

reason three

Why let wrong answers in?

Because people learn more where it is safe to be wrong. In my room a wrong answer is a clue about what the student is thinking, and nobody is embarrassed by one.

Edmondson, 1999 · Psychological safety (a study of work teams)

reason four

Why play it back instead of re-reading?

Because recalling something strengthens it more than reading it again. Games and quick challenges make students pull language out of their heads, which is the practice that sticks.

Roediger & Karpicke, 2006 · Retrieval practice

reason five

Why teach one task many ways?

Because the right help depends on the student. The same task reaches a quiet child, a competitive one and an artist differently, so I change the way in, not the goal.

Wood, Bruner & Ross, 1976 · Scaffolding
